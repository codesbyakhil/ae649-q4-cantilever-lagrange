# Theory, equations and analytical validation

**AE 649 course project: a Q4 plane-stress cantilever with the clamp imposed by Lagrange multipliers**

This document derives every equation the code uses, in order, and pairs every
numerical result with its analytical counterpart (V1–V6). Each `##` section is one or two slides.
Every number quoted here was produced by a script and can be found in the file named next to it
(`figures/*.txt`, regenerated by `python run_all.py`). Symbolic proofs of every closed form are in
`checks/derivations.py` (run `python -m checks.derivations`). Code references are `file:function`.

## 0. Problem and model

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Geometry | $x \in [0, L]$, $y \in [-D/2, D/2]$, thickness $t$; $L = 2.0$ m, $D = 0.15$ m, $t = 0.10$ m |
| Material | $E = 200$ GPa, $\nu = 0.30$, plane stress |
| Support | clamp at $x = 0$: $u = v = 0$ at every left-edge node, imposed by Lagrange multipliers |
| Load | $P = -10$ N (downward) as a uniform shear traction $\bar\tau = P/(tD)$ on the end face $x = L$ |
| Element | Q4, 2×2 Gauss, structured $n_x \times n_y$ meshes, $n_x = 3n_y$ |
| Output | tip deflection $v(L, 0)$, stresses, reactions $R = -C^T\lambda$ |

**Why plane stress.** The faces $z = \pm t/2$ are traction-free, and beam theory, our reference, also
assumes a uniaxial stress state ($\sigma_y = \sigma_z = 0$). Plane stress ($\sigma_z = \tau_{yz} = \tau_{zx} = 0$)
is therefore the 2D model consistent with the beam-theory comparison.

**Mesh sequence.** 3×1 → 6×2 → … → 384×128. Each mesh halves the element size of the previous
one (nested refinement), and every element has the same aspect ratio $a/b = (L/n_x)/(D/n_y) = 4.44$.

## 1. Plane-stress elasticity (strong form)

**Equilibrium** (no body force):
$$\frac{\partial \sigma_x}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial \tau_{xy}}{\partial y} = 0, \qquad
  \frac{\partial \tau_{xy}}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial \sigma_y}{\partial y} = 0$$

**Strain–displacement** (small strains, engineering shear strain):
$$\varepsilon_x = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x}, \qquad \varepsilon_y = \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}, \qquad
  \gamma_{xy} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial x}$$

**Hooke's law.** 3D isotropic Hooke's law with $\sigma_z = \tau_{yz} = \tau_{zx} = 0$ leaves
$$\varepsilon_x = \frac{\sigma_x - \nu\sigma_y}{E},\qquad \varepsilon_y = \frac{\sigma_y - \nu\sigma_x}{E},\qquad
  \gamma_{xy} = \frac{\tau_{xy}}{G},\qquad G = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}$$
Solving for the stresses:
$$\begin{Bmatrix}\sigma_x\\ \sigma_y\\ \tau_{xy}\end{Bmatrix} = \underbrace{\frac{E}{1-\nu^2}
  \begin{bmatrix}1 & \nu & 0\\ \nu & 1 & 0\\ 0 & 0 & \tfrac{1-\nu}{2}\end{bmatrix}}_{D}
  \begin{Bmatrix}\varepsilon_x\\ \varepsilon_y\\ \gamma_{xy}\end{Bmatrix}
  \qquad \left(\tfrac{E}{1-\nu^2}\cdot\tfrac{1-\nu}{2} = G\right)$$
(Cook Eq. 3.1-3.) Code: `fem/material.py:plane_stress_D`. Proof: `checks/derivations.py:plane_stress_matrix`
(inverts the 3D compliance symbolically). Test: uniaxial strain $[\sigma_0/E, -\nu\sigma_0/E, 0]$ gives
$[\sigma_0, 0, 0]$ (`tests/test_q4.py`).

**Boundary conditions.** $u = v = 0$ on $x = 0$; traction $(0, \bar\tau)$ on $x = L$; traction-free on $y = \pm D/2$.

## 2. Principle of minimum total potential energy

$$\Pi(u, v) = \frac12 \int_A \varepsilon^T D\, \varepsilon \; t\, dA \;-\; \int_{\Gamma_t} \mathbf{u}^T \bar{\mathbf{t}}\; t\, ds$$

Among displacement fields that satisfy the clamp, the exact solution makes $\Pi$ stationary
($\delta\Pi = 0$ is the weak form of equilibrium plus the traction conditions) and, because $D$ is
positive definite, a minimum.

**Discretisation.** With $\mathbf u \approx N \mathbf d$ in each element and $\varepsilon = B\mathbf d$:
$$\Pi(\mathbf d) = \tfrac12 \mathbf d^T K \mathbf d - \mathbf d^T \mathbf f, \qquad
  K = \sum_e \int_{A_e} B^T D B\, t\, dA, \qquad \mathbf f = \sum_e \int_{\Gamma_e} N^T \bar{\mathbf t}\, t\, ds$$
$\partial\Pi/\partial\mathbf d = 0$ gives $K\mathbf d = \mathbf f$ (once the supports are accounted for, §5).

**A consequence used later.** At the solution $\mathbf d^T K\mathbf d = \mathbf f^T\mathbf d$, so
$\Pi_{\min} = -\tfrac12 \mathbf f^T\mathbf d$: minimising $\Pi$ is maximising the compliance $\mathbf f^T\mathbf d$.

## 3. The Q4 element

### 3.1 Shape functions and isoparametric mapping

Parent square $-1 \le s, r \le 1$ ($s$ horizontal), nodes 1(−1,−1), 2(1,−1), 3(1,1), 4(−1,1), counter-clockwise:
$$N_i(s, r) = \tfrac14 (1 + s s_i)(1 + r r_i), \qquad x = \sum_i N_i x_i,\quad y = \sum_i N_i y_i,\quad
  u = \sum_i N_i u_i,\quad v = \sum_i N_i v_i$$
Properties (tested in `tests/test_q4.py`): $N_i(s_j, r_j) = \delta_{ij}$, $\sum_i N_i = 1$; "bilinear" because each
$N_i$ is a product of two 1D linear functions.

### 3.2 Jacobian, chain rule and the strain–displacement matrix

$$[J] = \begin{bmatrix} x_{,s} & y_{,s}\\ x_{,r} & y_{,r}\end{bmatrix}
      = \begin{bmatrix} N_{1,s} & \dots & N_{4,s}\\ N_{1,r} & \dots & N_{4,r}\end{bmatrix}
        \begin{bmatrix} x_1 & y_1\\ \vdots & \vdots\\ x_4 & y_4\end{bmatrix}, \qquad
  \begin{Bmatrix} N_{i,x}\\ N_{i,y}\end{Bmatrix} = [J]^{-1}\begin{Bmatrix} N_{i,s}\\ N_{i,r}\end{Bmatrix}$$
$$B = \begin{bmatrix} N_{1,x} & 0 & N_{2,x} & 0 & \dots\\ 0 & N_{1,y} & 0 & N_{2,y} & \dots\\
      N_{1,y} & N_{1,x} & N_{2,y} & N_{2,x} & \dots\end{bmatrix}, \qquad
  \mathbf d_e = [u_1, v_1, u_2, v_2, u_3, v_3, u_4, v_4]^T$$
$dA = \det J\, ds\, dr$, and $\det J > 0$ is required (the code raises an error otherwise:
clockwise node order or a badly distorted element). For a rectangle $2a \times 2b$: $J = \mathrm{diag}(a, b)$,
$\det J = ab$ = area/4 (tested).

Code: `fem/q4.py:shape_functions, shape_derivatives, jacobian, strain_displacement`.

### 3.3 Element stiffness and Gauss quadrature

$$k = t\int_{-1}^{1}\!\!\int_{-1}^{1} B^T D B \,\det J\; ds\, dr \;\approx\; t \sum_{i=1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{2} B^T D B\,\det J\; w_i w_j,
  \qquad s_i, r_j = \pm\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3},\; w = 1$$
An $n$-point Gauss rule integrates polynomials up to degree $2n-1$ exactly, so 2 points are exact to degree 3.

**Why 2×2 Gauss is exact for a rectangle.** With $x = a s$, $y = b r$:
$N_{i,x} = s_i (1 + r r_i)/(4a)$ is linear in $r$ only and $N_{i,y} = r_i(1 + s s_i)/(4b)$ is linear in $s$ only;
$\det J = ab$ is constant. Every entry of $B^TDB$ is a product of two such terms: at most quadratic in $s$ and
at most quadratic in $r$. Degree 2 ≤ 3, so the rule is exact. For a general quadrilateral $\det J$ varies,
$J^{-1}$ makes the integrand rational, and Gauss quadrature is only approximate.

**Closed form (V2).** Integrating by hand (Cook §3.6), e.g.
$$k_{11} = \frac{Et}{1-\nu^2}\left[\frac{b}{3a} + \frac{(1-\nu)a}{6b}\right], \qquad k_{12} = \frac{Et}{8(1-\nu)}$$
All 64 entries are in `checks/closed_form.py:q4_rectangle_stiffness` (integrals
$\int N_{i,x}N_{j,x}\,dA = \tfrac{b}{4a}s_is_j(1 + \tfrac{r_ir_j}{3})$, $\int N_{i,y}N_{j,y}\,dA = \tfrac{a}{4b}r_ir_j(1 + \tfrac{s_is_j}{3})$,
$\int N_{i,x}N_{j,y}\,dA = \tfrac{s_ir_j}{4}$). Note that $k$ depends only on $a/b$, not on the element size.

Code: `fem/q4.py:element_stiffness` (one element, or a whole stack of elements at once with the same formulas).

### 3.4 Rigid-body modes, rank and completeness

* **Zero-energy modes.** Translations and the small rotation $u = -\theta y,\ v = \theta x$ give
  $\varepsilon_x = \varepsilon_y = 0$, $\gamma_{xy} = -\theta + \theta = 0$, hence $k\,\mathbf d_{\text{rigid}} = 0$: three zero eigenvalues.
  With 2×2 Gauss the rank is $8 - 3 = 5$ (no spurious modes). One Gauss point would give rank at most 3,
  i.e. two extra zero-energy "hourglass" modes — the reason full (2×2) integration is used.
* **Completeness.** Because $\sum N_i = 1$, $\sum N_i x_i = x$, $\sum N_i y_i = y$ on *any* quadrilateral,
  every linear displacement field is represented exactly, so every constant-strain state is exact. This is
  the basis of the patch test (V3).
* **Why the patch test also passes with 2×2 Gauss on distorted elements.** $\det J\, N_{i,x}$ and
  $\det J\, N_{i,y}$ equal $\mathrm{adj}(J)[N_{i,s}, N_{i,r}]^T$, which is bilinear in $(s, r)$. For a constant stress
  the nodal forces $t\int B^T\sigma \det J\,ds\,dr$ are therefore integrated exactly, they equal the consistent
  loads of the boundary tractions, and the exact linear field satisfies the discrete equations exactly.

## 4. Assembly and consistent loads

**Assembly.** Node $n$ (0-based) owns global DOFs $2n$ ($u$) and $2n+1$ ($v$).
$$K = \sum_e L_e^T\, k_e\, L_e$$
where $L_e$ picks the element's 8 DOFs out of the global vector. In the code every $k_e$ is written as
64 (row, column, value) triplets; converting the COO list to CSR adds the duplicates, which *is* the sum above.
Code: `fem/assemble.py:element_dofs, assemble_stiffness`. Mesh numbering: `fem/mesh.py` (nodes up each
vertical grid line; element corners counter-clockwise, matching the parent nodes).

**Consistent load of a uniform traction.** Along an element edge of length $h$ the shape functions are
linear, $N_1 = (1-\xi)/2$, $N_2 = (1+\xi)/2$, $ds = (h/2)\,d\xi$:
$$f_i = t\int N_i\,\bar\tau\,ds = \frac{t h \bar\tau}{2}\quad\text{at each end node of the edge}$$
For the end load, $\bar\tau = P/(tD)$ on $n_y$ edges of length $D/n_y$: **$P/n_y$ at every interior end node,
$P/(2n_y)$ at the two corners**, summing to $P$ (tested). Code: `fem/loads.py:edge_traction_load`.

**Tip deflection** $v(L, 0)$: the mid-height end node when $n_y$ is even; for $n_y = 1$, linear interpolation
along the edge (the FE field is linear there), i.e. the mean of the two end nodes. Code: `fem/post.py:tip_deflection`.

## 5. The clamp by Lagrange multipliers

### 5.1 Augmented functional and saddle-point system

Constraints, one row per clamped DOF: $C\mathbf d = \mathbf g$ ($m = 2(n_y+1)$ rows; the clamp has $\mathbf g = 0$;
V1 and V3 also use $\mathbf g \ne 0$). Each row of $C$ holds a single 1 in the column of its DOF.
$$\Pi^*(\mathbf d, \boldsymbol\lambda) = \tfrac12\mathbf d^TK\mathbf d - \mathbf d^T\mathbf f + \boldsymbol\lambda^T(C\mathbf d - \mathbf g)$$
$$\frac{\partial\Pi^*}{\partial\mathbf d} = 0:\; K\mathbf d + C^T\boldsymbol\lambda = \mathbf f, \qquad
  \frac{\partial\Pi^*}{\partial\boldsymbol\lambda} = 0:\; C\mathbf d = \mathbf g
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
  \begin{bmatrix}K & C^T\\ C & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{Bmatrix}\mathbf d\\ \boldsymbol\lambda\end{Bmatrix} =
  \begin{Bmatrix}\mathbf f\\ \mathbf g\end{Bmatrix}$$
Size $n + m$. All DOFs stay in the system; $K$ alone is singular (three rigid-body modes), the
augmented matrix is not. Code: `fem/lagrange.py:constraint_matrix, augmented_matrix, solve_lagrange`.

### 5.2 Why the matrix is indefinite (no Cholesky) and when it is nonsingular

* **Indefinite.** For $\mathbf x = [\mathbf 0; \boldsymbol\mu]$: $\mathbf x^TA\mathbf x = 0$ with $\mathbf x \ne 0$. For
  $\mathbf x = [C^T\boldsymbol\mu;\, -\alpha\boldsymbol\mu]$: $\mathbf x^TA\mathbf x = \boldsymbol\mu^TCKC^T\boldsymbol\mu - 2\alpha\,|C^T\boldsymbol\mu|^2 < 0$
  for large $\alpha$. So $A$ is not positive definite and Cholesky does not apply; we use sparse LU with
  pivoting (`scipy.sparse.linalg.spsolve`, SuperLU, minimum-degree ordering on $A + A^T$).
* **Nonsingular** when $C$ has full row rank (distinct DOFs) and $K$ is positive definite on the null space of
  $C$ (the clamp removes all three rigid-body modes). Then $A$ has exactly $n$ positive and $m$ negative
  eigenvalues (inertia of a saddle-point matrix). Tested on 3×1, 6×2 and 12×4
  (`tests/test_cantilever.py`).

### 5.3 The multipliers are the reactions

The first block row is $K\mathbf d + C^T\boldsymbol\lambda = \mathbf f$. Equilibrium of the discrete structure is
$K\mathbf d = \mathbf f + \mathbf R$, where $\mathbf R$ are the support reactions. Hence
$$\boxed{\mathbf R = K\mathbf d - \mathbf f = -C^T\boldsymbol\lambda}$$
Each row of $C$ picks one DOF, so the reaction at the $i$-th clamped DOF is $-\lambda_i$. Physically, $\lambda_i$ is the
force the constraint must exert (with a minus sign) to enforce $C\mathbf d = \mathbf g$: the multipliers deliver
the reactions without any post-processing.

### 5.4 Equivalence with elimination

Order the DOFs as free ($f$) and constrained ($c$), so $C = [0\ \ I]$. The second row gives $\mathbf d_c = \mathbf g$.
The first row splits into
$$K_{ff}\mathbf d_f = \mathbf f_f - K_{fc}\mathbf g \quad\text{(the eliminated system)}, \qquad
  \boldsymbol\lambda = \mathbf f_c - K_{cf}\mathbf d_f - K_{cc}\mathbf g = -\mathbf R_c$$
So Lagrange multipliers give exactly the elimination displacements, plus the reactions. Proved
symbolically for a generic $3\times3$ $K$ (`checks/derivations.py:lagrange_equivalence`); checked numerically in
`tests/test_bar1d.py` and `tests/test_cantilever.py` (elimination is written only inside the tests).

### 5.5 Scaling the constraint rows (conditioning)

$K$ has entries of order $10^{10}$ N/m while $C$ holds ones. Multiply the constraint rows *and* columns by
$s = \max\operatorname{diag}K$:
$$\begin{bmatrix}K & sC^T\\ sC & 0\end{bmatrix}\begin{Bmatrix}\mathbf d\\ \boldsymbol\lambda_s\end{Bmatrix} =
  \begin{Bmatrix}\mathbf f\\ s\mathbf g\end{Bmatrix}, \qquad A_s = SAS,\quad S = \mathrm{diag}(I, sI)$$
The solution is $S^{-1}[\mathbf d; \boldsymbol\lambda]$: **$\mathbf d$ is unchanged, $\boldsymbol\lambda_s = \boldsymbol\lambda/s$**, and
$\mathbf R = -(sC)^T\boldsymbol\lambda_s = -C^T\boldsymbol\lambda$. The second row $sC\mathbf d = s\mathbf g$ is the same constraint.
$A_s = SAS$ is a congruence, so the inertia (§5.2) is unchanged (Sylvester's law of inertia).

**Why the unscaled matrix is so badly conditioned.** With $C = [0\ I]$ the exact inverse of $A$ contains the
block $K_{cf}K_{ff}^{-1}K_{fc} - K_{cc}$ (minus the stiffness the supports see), of the size of $K$ itself. So
$\|A\|\,\|A^{-1}\| \sim \|K\|^2 \sim 10^{22}$. With scaling that block is divided by $s^2$ and the coupling blocks by
$s$, so $\operatorname{cond}(A_s) \approx \operatorname{cond}(K_{ff})$, the conditioning of the eliminated system.

Measured (`figures/exact_field.txt`, float64 SVD, confirmed by a 50-digit SVD on 3×1 and 6×2):

| mesh | cond₂, unscaled | cond₂, scaled | cond₂($K_{ff}$) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3×1 | 9.381e21 | 2.492e4 | 2.412e4 |
| 6×2 | 2.381e22 | 2.746e5 | 2.728e5 |
| 12×4 | 3.594e22 | 1.921e6 | 1.919e6 |
| 24×8 | 4.170e22 | 9.140e6 | 9.139e6 |

Cook §9.3 (Eq. 9.3-4): about $\log_{10}\operatorname{cond}$ digits may be lost. Unscaled that exceeds the 16 digits
of double precision; scaled it is 4–7. Effect on the exact linear field (V3, 12×4,
`figures/exact_field.txt`): relative nodal error **4.4e-10 unscaled vs 3.5e-14 scaled** (regular mesh) and
**2.5e-9 vs 1.1e-13** (distorted mesh): about four digits lost without scaling, even though the residual
stays small. Beyond cond₂ ≈ 1/eps the smallest eigenvalues are not reliably computable in float64: on 12×4
`eigvalsh` of the unscaled matrix returns 134 positive / 6 negative eigenvalues instead of 130 / 10; the scaled
matrix gives 130 / 10, as theory requires (`figures/exact_field.txt`).

## 6. Beam-theory reference

Moment at section $x$ (sagging positive): $M(x) = P(L - x)$.

* **Euler–Bernoulli:** $EI v'' = M$, $v(0) = v'(0) = 0$ ⇒ $v = \frac{P}{EI}\left(\frac{Lx^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{6}\right)$,
  $$v(L) = \frac{PL^3}{3EI}, \qquad I = \int_{-D/2}^{D/2} y^2\, t\, dy = \frac{tD^3}{12} = 2.8125\times10^{-5}\ \text{m}^4$$
* **Bending stress:** $\sigma_{xx} = -\dfrac{M y}{I} = -\dfrac{P(L-x)\,y}{I}$ (top fibre in tension for $P < 0$).
* **Shear stress** from equilibrium $\partial\sigma_{xx}/\partial x + \partial\tau_{xy}/\partial y = 0$ with $\tau_{xy}(\pm D/2) = 0$:
  $\tau_{xy} = \dfrac{P}{2I}\left(\dfrac{D^2}{4} - y^2\right)$, whose resultant $\int\tau_{xy}t\,dy = P$.
* **Timoshenko:** shear strain $V/(\kappa GA)$ with $V = P$, $A = tD$, $\kappa = 5/6$ for a rectangle (Cook p. 535):
  $$v(L) = \frac{PL^3}{3EI} + \frac{PL}{\kappa G A}$$

Values (`python -m checks.beam_theory`): Euler–Bernoulli **−4.74074 µm**, shear term **−0.02080 µm**,
Timoshenko **−4.76154 µm**; $\sigma_{xx}(L/2, \pm D/2) = \pm 26.6667$ kPa.
All of these are derived symbolically in `checks/derivations.py:beam_theory`.

Beam theory needs only the resultant $P$; mid-span ($x = L/2$, 6.7 depths from either end) is far enough from
the load and the clamp for Saint-Venant end effects to have decayed, so stresses are compared there.

## 7. Why Q4 is stiff in bending (Cook Eq. 3.6-11, V4)

A block $2a \times 2b$ in pure bending has (Cook Eq. 3.6-9)
$\varepsilon_x = -\theta y/(2a)$, $\varepsilon_y = \nu\theta y/(2a)$, $\gamma_{xy} = 0$.
A Q4 element bent the same way keeps straight sides; each node moves horizontally by $\mp\theta b/2$, so
(Eq. 3.6-10) $\varepsilon_x = -\theta y/(2a)$ (exact), $\varepsilon_y = 0$, $\gamma_{xy} = -\theta x/(2a)$ — a **parasitic shear**
that should be zero. Strain energies (with plane-stress $D$):
$$U_b = \frac{E t\, \theta^2 b^3}{6a}, \qquad
  U_{el} = \frac{t\,\theta^2 b}{6a}\left[\frac{E b^2}{1-\nu^2} + G a^2\right]$$
At equal moment $U = M\theta/2 = k\theta^2/2$, so $\theta_{el}/\theta_b = k_b/k_{el} = U_b/U_{el}$:
$$\boxed{\frac{\theta_{FE}}{\theta_{exact}} = \frac{1-\nu^2}{1 + \frac{1-\nu}{2}\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^2}}
\qquad\text{(Cook Eq. 3.6-11, p. 99)}$$
The factor $1-\nu^2$ comes from $\varepsilon_y = 0$; the $(a/b)^2$ term only from parasitic shear (**shear locking**).

**FE check** (`figures/bending_error.txt`): one clamped element under an end couple, and $n_x\times1$ strips of
the cantilever (every element carries the same moment, so the strip behaves exactly like one element with
$a = L/2n_x$). FE and formula agree to within 8.6e-13 for every case; e.g. $a/b = 1$: 0.6740740741;
3×1 strip ($a/b = 4.44$): 0.1149921997. In pure bending the 3×1 strip rotates only 11.5% as much as the exact
solution — the reason the coarse meshes in §8 are so stiff.

## 8. Convergence theory

* **Monotone compliance (minimum potential energy).** Each mesh subdivides the previous one, so its
  displacement space contains the previous one ($V_h \subset V_{h/2}$; the coarse clamp, $u = v = 0$ at the
  coarse left nodes, is also zero along the whole edge, so it satisfies the fine clamp). Minimising $\Pi$ over a
  larger space cannot give a larger minimum: $\Pi_{h/2} \le \Pi_h$, and with $\Pi_{\min} = -\tfrac12\mathbf f^T\mathbf d$,
  **the compliance $\mathbf f^T\mathbf d$ can only increase**, towards the exact value from below (the FE model is
  too stiff). Proven for the compliance; the tip deflection itself is observed to be monotone too.
* **Rate.** For bilinear elements and a smooth solution the energy-norm error is $O(h)$ and displacements
  (and output functionals like the tip deflection) converge as $O(h^2)$.
* **Observed order** from three nested meshes, $\phi = \phi_\infty + c h^q$:
  $$\frac{\phi_1 - \phi_2}{\phi_2 - \phi_3} = 2^q \quad\Rightarrow\quad q = \log_2\frac{\phi_1 - \phi_2}{\phi_2 - \phi_3}$$
* **Richardson extrapolation** (Cook Eq. 9.7-1, valid for regular nested refinement with the quantity at a
  fixed node — both hold here):
  $$\phi_\infty = \frac{2^q\phi_{h/2} - \phi_h}{2^q - 1}$$

Results (`figures/tip_deflection.txt`): observed order 1.461 (12×4→48×16), 1.829, 1.938, **1.961**
(96×32→384×128), rising towards 2. Coarse meshes are pre-asymptotic: locking dominates there (§7).
Richardson on the finest triple: **−4.75532 µm** (changes by 0.004% from the previous triple's estimate).

**A caveat to state honestly.** The two clamped corners are singular points of the 2D elasticity solution:
the reaction traction at a corner keeps growing under refinement (`figures/reactions.txt`). A corner singularity
can lower the asymptotic rate below 2 on very fine meshes; over the meshes used here the observed order is
still rising towards 2, so its effect on the tip deflection is not visible.

## 9. The validations, one slide each

### V1 — 1D bar, both ends prescribed (Lagrange machinery alone)
$EAu'' + q = 0$, $u(0) = 0$, $u(L) = U$: exact $u = \frac{qx(L-x)}{2EA} + \frac{Ux}{L}$; support forces
$-N(0) = -\frac{qL}{2} - \frac{EAU}{L}$ and $N(L) = -\frac{qL}{2} + \frac{EAU}{L}$ ($L = 1$ m, $EA = 2\times10^7$ N, $q = 1000$ N/m,
$U = 10^{-5}$ m). Linear elements with constant $EA$ and consistent loads are **nodally exact** (Cook p. 47): the
Green's function of a nodal point load is piecewise linear, so it lies in the FE space and Galerkin orthogonality
makes the nodal error zero. Result (`figures/bar1d.txt`): nodal error ≤ 4.8e-15 (relative) on 2, 3, 8, 32 elements;
$-\lambda = -700$ N and $-300$ N, equal to the exact support forces. Uses the same solver as the 2D problem.

### V2 — Q4 element stiffness
Code vs exact integration (§3.3), `figures/element_checks.txt`: all 64 entries agree to ≤ 2.3e-16 (relative);
unit square $k_{11} = 9.8901098901\times10^9$ N/m, $k_{12} = 3.5714285714\times10^9$ N/m; 2×2 and 3×3 Gauss agree to
≤ 7.7e-16 for rectangles but differ by 8.3e-3 for a general quadrilateral; exactly 3 zero eigenvalues
($|\lambda|/\lambda_{\max} \le 1.3\times10^{-16}$).

### V3 — Uniform tension: exact linear field (patch test)
$u = \sigma_0 x/E$, $v = -\nu\sigma_0 y/E$ gives $\sigma = [\sigma_0, 0, 0]$, traction-free top and bottom, $u = 0$ on $x = 0$,
$v = 0$ at the left mid-height node (all supports by Lagrange multipliers). Q4 contains every linear field
(§3.4), so the FE solution must be exact on any mesh. Result (`figures/exact_field.txt`, 12×4): nodal error
3.5e-14 (regular) and 1.1e-13 (randomly distorted), Gauss-point stresses uniform to ≤ 4.9e-14, reactions equal the
consistent loads of $\sigma_0$ on the left edge with opposite sign. The tests also prescribe the exact field plus a
rigid motion (non-zero and non-constant $\mathbf g$): reproduced to round-off.

### V4 — Q4 in pure bending
Cook Eq. 3.6-11 (§7); FE equals the formula to ≤ 8.6e-13 for single elements ($a/b$ = 0.5 to 6) and strips
(1×1 to 24×1). `figures/bending_error.txt`, `figures/bending_error.png`.

### V5 — One-element cantilever, solved fully by hand
Symmetry (u odd, v even in y) leaves two unknowns, $u_3 = -u_2 = q$ and $v_2 = v_3 = w$, with
$u = qr(1+s)/2$, $v = w(1+s)/2$, $\varepsilon_x = qr/2a$, $\varepsilon_y = 0$, $\gamma_{xy} = q(1+s)/2b + w/2a$.
Minimising $\Pi(q, w) = U - Pw$:
$$v_{tip} = \frac{4PL(1+\nu)\left[2L^2(1-\nu) + D^2\right]}{E t D\left[L^2(1-\nu) + 2D^2\right]}, \qquad
  u_{top} = -u_{bottom} = -\frac{6PL^2(1-\nu^2)}{Et\left[L^2(1-\nu) + 2D^2\right]}$$
Reactions: $\sum F_x = 0$ and $\sum M_O = 0$ give $R_x = -PL/D$ (bottom), $+PL/D$ (top); $\sum F_y = 0$ gives
$R_{y,bottom} + R_{y,top} = -P$, and symmetry splits it equally, $-P/2$ each. (`checks/derivations.py` re-derives all of
this over the four free DOFs without assuming symmetry.)
Result (`figures/one_element.txt`): $v_{tip} = -6.8510837727\times10^{-8}$ m, $u_{top} = +3.8383128295\times10^{-9}$ m,
$R_x = +133.333$ N (bottom) and $-133.333$ N (top), $R_y = +5.000$ N each, code = hand solution to ≤ 1.5e-13,
and $\boldsymbol\lambda = -\mathbf R$ at the four clamped DOFs. One element gives 0.01445 of the Euler–Bernoulli deflection:
locking with $a/b = 13.3$.

### V6 — The real problem on 3×1 … 384×128
(a) **Statics** (`figures/reactions.txt`): $\sum R_x = 0$, $\sum R_y = -P = 10$ N, reaction moment $-PL = 20$ N·m, net
moment of reactions and loads zero — on every mesh to round-off (worst: $\sum R_y$ off by 1.9e-8 N on 384×128).
Reason: element stiffnesses annihilate rigid-body motions, so $K\mathbf d$ is self-equilibrated.
(b) **Clapeyron** $\mathbf d^TK\mathbf d = \mathbf f^T\mathbf d$ (from $K\mathbf d + C^T\boldsymbol\lambda = \mathbf f$ and $C\mathbf d = 0$): relative

(c) **Monotone compliance** under nested refinement: true on every mesh (`figures/tip_deflection.txt`).

## 10. Conclusions and limitations

* **Lagrange multipliers work exactly as the theory says**: same displacements as elimination, and the
  multipliers *are* the reactions ($\mathbf R = -C^T\boldsymbol\lambda$), with statics satisfied to round-off. They also
  handle non-zero prescribed values ($\mathbf g \ne 0$) with no change to the code.
* **Scaling is essential**: unscaled, cond₂ ≈ 1e22 and about four digits are lost on an exact field; scaling the
  constraint rows and columns by $\max\operatorname{diag}K$ makes the system as well conditioned as the eliminated one.
* **Q4 locks in bending**: one element gives 0.01445 of the Euler–Bernoulli deflection and 3×1 gives 0.1155;
  in pure bending the error is predicted exactly by Cook Eq. 3.6-11. Fine meshes are needed (within 1% of
  Timoshenko from 96×32 on).
* **Converged answer**: −4.75532 µm = **0.99869 × Timoshenko (−0.13%)** and **1.00308 × Euler–Bernoulli
  (+0.31%)**. Beam theory is not the exact 2D solution: it ignores the restraint of cross-section warping and
  Poisson contraction at a fully clamped root and the local effect of how the end load is distributed. These are
  2D effects that scale like $(D/L)^2$, the same order as the shear correction itself
  ($E/(4\kappa G)\,(D/L)^2$ = 0.44% of Euler–Bernoulli here, `python -m checks.beam_theory`), so a 0.1–0.3% gap is
  what to expect. The exact 2D solution for this clamp is outside the project scope.
* **Reactions along the clamp**: $R_x$ follows the beam-theory distribution $PLy/I$ (interior nodes within 5.4% on
  96×32); the clamped corners carry a stress concentration that grows under refinement, and the $y$ reactions are
  not parabolic at a fully clamped 2D edge — beam theory fixes only their resultant there (Saint-Venant).
* **Limitations**: linear elasticity, small displacements, plane stress, one element type (Q4), structured meshes,
  one load case; the root-corner singularity is not resolved (stresses there are mesh-dependent).

## Appendix B — What `checks/derivations.py` proves (sympy)

Plane-stress $D$ from 3D Hooke's law; exact $k$ of a rectangle and that 2×2 Gauss reproduces it exactly; the
$k_{11}$, $k_{12}$ formulas; Cook Eq. 3.6-11 from the two strain energies; the one-element cantilever (all

the bar solution, its ODE, end conditions and support forces; the uniform-tension field and its stresses; the
consistent edge load $th\bar\tau/2$; Lagrange = elimination, $\lambda = -R$, and $\lambda_s = \lambda/s$ under scaling;
Richardson's formula and $(\phi_1-\phi_2)/(\phi_2-\phi_3) = 2^q$.

## Appendix C — Notes on the results

* **V5 reactions:** $R_x$ follows from statics alone ($\sum F_x$, $\sum M$), but the split of $R_y$ between the two clamped
  nodes does not (two unknowns at the same $x$, one equation); it follows from $\sum F_y$ **plus symmetry** (confirmed
  by the full four-DOF solution). $u_{tip}$ is $+3.838313\times10^{-9}$ m at the top node and $-3.838313\times10^{-9}$ m at the
  bottom node.
* **Scaling:** on the 12×4 exact field the nodal error is 4.4e-10 unscaled vs 3.5e-14 scaled (regular mesh) and
  2.5e-9 vs 1.1e-13 (distorted): about four digits lost without scaling; cond₂ 3.594e22 → 1.921e6.
* **Mid-span stress error measure:** each Gauss point's error is normalised by the beam surface stress at that
  point's own $x$, over the element column containing $L/2$ (the column just left of $L/2$ when $L/2$ is a grid line).
  The error rises slightly from 3×1 to 6×2 and falls after.
* **Cook §9.3** is about condition numbers and digit loss (Eq. 9.3-4) and diagonal scaling (Eq. 9.3-2); that
  scaling leaves $\mathbf d$ unchanged is the congruence argument of §5.5.
